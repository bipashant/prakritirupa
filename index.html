Chapter 1 · She Who Is Nature

The mountain, the river, the field in flower. All of it is her.

She Who Is Nature tells the story of DurgaThe Mother Goddess. Her name means “hard to reach,” like a fort high on a mountain., the great Mother Goddess, and the forms she takes during the nine nights of Navaratri“Nine nights.” An autumn festival honouring the Goddess, one form each night., celebrated in Nepal as DashainNepal's biggest festival. It is the same festival as Navaratri and Vijaya Dashami, and ends in victory on the tenth day.. In this telling she lives close by, as PrakritiSanskrit for nature: the living world, in all its moods., nature itself: the ground under our feet, the rain and the fire, and the quiet strength inside every living thing.

Each chapter brings one of her forms to life with a story, an image of her, and her mantra in Sanskrit with simple pronunciation and meaning. You don't need to know anything about Hindu tradition to enjoy it.

Chapter 2 · A Mountain of Fire Becomes a Woman

When the gods had nothing left, their light became her.

After a war of a hundred years, the buffalo demon MahishasuraThe shape-shifting buffalo demon who took over heaven. threw IndraKing of the gods, ruler of heaven. off the throne of heaven and drove the gods out to wander the earth. When VishnuThe god who keeps the world going, one of the three great gods. and ShivaThe god of endings that make way for new beginnings, and Parvati's husband. heard, their faces darkened with anger. A great light poured from Vishnu's face, then from BrahmaThe creator god, one of the three great gods of the Hindu tradition., from Shiva and from everyone there, and it all flowed together like rivers running to the sea.

The light became a mountain of fire, and slowly the fire took a woman's shape. Shiva gave her a trident, Vishnu a discus, Indra his thunderbolt, and the king of the mountains a lion. She laughed, and the mountains shook.

This story comes from the Devi MahatmyaAn old Sanskrit poem in praise of the Goddess, still recited during Dashain., an old Sanskrit poem still recited during DashainNepal's biggest festival. It is the same festival as Navaratri and Vijaya Dashami, and ends in victory on the tenth day..

Navaratri“Nine nights.” An autumn festival honouring the Goddess, one form each night. · nine nights

Nine nights, nine forms

Her nine names come from a short old prayer, the Devi Kavacham“Armor of the Goddess.” A short protective prayer that names the nine forms., said to have been spoken by BrahmaThe creator god, one of the three great gods of the Hindu tradition. himself. Over the nine nights of Navaratri they are honoured one at a time, like lamps lit one after another, from the daughter of the mountain to the giver of every gift. Night 1

Shailaputriशैलपुत्री

Rides a white bull · holds a trident and a lotus · a half-moon in her hair

In an earlier life she was SatiShiva's first wife, who gave up her life. She is later born again as Parvati.. When her father, King Daksha, mocked ShivaThe god of endings that make way for new beginnings, and Parvati's husband. in front of everyone at his great sacred fire, she let her body go. She was born again to HimavatThe king of the mountains, father of Parvati. The Himalaya itself., king of the Himalaya, and his queen Mena, who named her Parvati“She of the mountain.” Daughter of the Himalaya and wife of Shiva, and the Goddess in a human story., the mountain's daughter.

She has already lost everything once, and she has come back smiling. The nine forms begin with her: with a mountain, and a return.

Her mantraॐ देवी शैलपुत्र्यै नमःOm Devi Shailaputryai NamahOm, I bow to the Goddess Shailaputri.

Night 2

Brahmachariniब्रह्मचारिणी

Walks barefoot · holds prayer beads and a water pot

ShivaThe god of endings that make way for new beginnings, and Parvati's husband. was still grieving for SatiShiva's first wife, who gave up her life. She is later born again as Parvati. and noticed no one, so the young Parvati“She of the mountain.” Daughter of the Himalaya and wife of Shiva, and the Goddess in a human story. set out to become as strong in spirit as he was. She sat between fires in summer and stood in icy water on winter nights. She ate only fruit, then fallen leaves, then not even leaves, and people called her Aparna, “the one without even a leaf.”

At last a young student came to mock Shiva, and she angrily sent him away. He smiled and showed his true face. It was Shiva himself: “From today, I am yours.”

Her mantraॐ देवी ब्रह्मचारिण्यै नमःOm Devi Brahmacharinyai NamahOm, I bow to the Goddess Brahmacharini.

Night 3

Chandraghantaचन्द्रघण्टा

Rides a lion · ten arms · a bell-shaped half-moon on her brow

Her name means “moon bell,” for the bell-shaped half-moon on her forehead. No old story carries her name, so the tales told about her are newer ones. In one, ShivaThe god of endings that make way for new beginnings, and Parvati's husband. arrives at the wedding with ghosts and wild hermits, and Parvati“She of the mountain.” Daughter of the Himalaya and wife of Shiva, and the Goddess in a human story.'s mother faints. Parvati takes this fierce form on her lion and stands between her family and the wedding party until Shiva agrees to come as a bridegroom should.

In another, the ring of her moon bell scatters an army of giant bats. Her face stays calm, almost gentle. She is ready for battle; she just isn't angry about it.

Her mantraॐ देवी चन्द्रघण्टायै नमःOm Devi Chandraghantayai NamahOm, I bow to the Goddess Chandraghanta.

Night 4

Kushmandaकूष्माण्डा

Rides a tiger · eight arms · lives at the heart of the sun

Her name is often read as ku, little, ushma, warmth, and anda, egg: the little warm egg the whole universe grew from. Before anything existed there was only darkness. Then she smiled, and the light of that smile broke the warm egg of the world open into stars and planets and suns.

Ever since, they say, she has lived at the heart of the sun. When the sun warms your face on a cold morning, you could think of it as her smile, still reaching you.

Her mantraॐ देवी कूष्माण्डायै नमःOm Devi Kushmandayai NamahOm, I bow to the Goddess Kushmanda.

Night 5

Skandamataस्कन्दमाता

Sits on a lotus · a lion resting beside her · holds baby SkandaA young warrior god, son of Shiva and Parvati. Also called Kartikeya or Murugan. and two lotus flowers

Only a son of ShivaThe god of endings that make way for new beginnings, and Parvati's husband. could defeat the demon TarakaA demon who could be defeated only by a son of Shiva.. That son, Skanda, also called KartikeyaAnother name for Skanda, the young warrior god. or MuruganAnother name for Skanda, loved especially in South India., was carried into the world by fire, by the river GangaThe Ganges, India's most sacred river, itself honoured as a goddess. and by six star-maidens, the Pleiades, and grew six faces to drink from all six. Then Parvati“She of the mountain.” Daughter of the Himalaya and wife of Shiva, and the Goddess in a human story. lifted him onto her lap and fed him as her own.

When he was grown, she gave him a spear called ShaktiDivine power. Also a name for the Goddess herself., her own power in the shape of a weapon, and with it he set the heavens free. She gave him more than milk. She gave him her strength.

Her mantraॐ देवी स्कन्दमातायै नमःOm Devi Skandamatayai NamahOm, I bow to the Goddess Skandamata.

Night 6

Katyayaniकात्यायनी

Rides a lion · holds a crescent sword and a lotus

Her name means “daughter of Katyayana,” a wise old hermit. Some say she appeared in his forest home; others say she took his name because he was the first to pray to her. In one telling she is the goddess born from the gods' anger. When the buffalo demon asked to marry her, she told him to beat her in battle first, then leapt from her lion onto his back and struck him down.

Her other story is gentler. In a village by the river YamunaA sacred river in northern India, beside the village where Krishna grew up., girls in love with KrishnaA beloved god, shown as a cowherd boy who plays the flute. bathed in the cold river before dawn for a whole month and prayed to her to make him their husband. Young women still pray to her when they hope to find the right person to marry.

Her mantraॐ देवी कात्यायन्यै नमःOm Devi Katyayanyai NamahOm, I bow to the Goddess Katyayani.

Night 7

Kalaratriकालरात्रि

Rides a donkey · dark skin, wild hair, fire in her breath

Kala means both black and time, and ratri means night, so she is the Black Night, the Night of Time. She is the form people fear most, and she means to be. In a story long told about her, she drinks every drop of the demon RaktabijaA demon whose every drop of blood became another demon.'s blood before it can touch the ground and rise up as another demon.

Yet she is also called Shubhankari, “the one who brings good.” Two of her four hands bless and say “don't be afraid.” She never harms those who come to her. She only frightens the things that frighten them.

Her mantraॐ देवी कालरात्र्यै नमःOm Devi Kalaratryai NamahOm, I bow to the Goddess Kalaratri.

Night 8

Mahagauriमहागौरी

Rides a white bull · holds a trident and a small drum

Three stories tell how she became so bright, and they don't agree. The one most people tell today is the simplest: after years of hard practice in the sun, rain and dust, Parvati“She of the mountain.” Daughter of the Himalaya and wife of Shiva, and the Goddess in a human story.'s body had turned dark. When ShivaThe god of endings that make way for new beginnings, and Parvati's husband. finally accepted her, he washed her with water from the river GangaThe Ganges, India's most sacred river, itself honoured as a goddess., and her skin shone white as lightning.

She comes after the long dark of Kalaratri and feels like rest, like the first clear morning after the rains, when everything looks new.

Her mantraॐ देवी महागौर्यै नमःOm Devi Mahagauryai NamahOm, I bow to the Goddess Mahagauri.

Night 9

Siddhidatriसिद्धिदात्री

Sits on a lotus · a lion beside her · holds a discus, conch, mace and lotus

A siddhi is a perfection, a mastery that comes after long practice. She holds them all and gives them away. Sages, heavenly musicians, forest spirits and even demons gather around her with folded hands, and nobody is turned away.

When ShivaThe god of endings that make way for new beginnings, and Parvati's husband. asked her for the power to create, she gave him every gift she had, and then became half of him. That is Ardhanarishvara“The lord who is half woman.” Shiva shown half man, half woman, joined down the middle., “the lord who is half woman.” With her, the nine nights are complete.

Her mantraॐ देवी सिद्धिदात्र्यै नमःOm Devi Siddhidatryai NamahOm, I bow to the Goddess Siddhidatri.

The tenth day · Vijaya Dashami“Victory tenth.” The tenth day of the festival, when Durga's victory is celebrated.

MahishasuraThe shape-shifting buffalo demon who took over heaven. could become anything: a buffalo, a lion, a man with a sword, an elephant. She did not chase every shape. She held him still, and when his true face came crawling half out of the buffalo, she ended it, while flowers fell from the clouds.
