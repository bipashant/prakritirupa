Chapter 1 · The Mother who is nature

The mountain, the river, the field in flower. All of it is her.

She Who Is Nature tells the story of Durga, the great Mother Goddess, and the forms she takes during the nine nights of Navaratri, celebrated in Nepal as Dashain. In this telling she lives close by, as Prakriti, nature itself: the ground under our feet, the rain and the fire, and the quiet strength inside every living thing.

Each chapter brings one of her forms to life with a story, a full painting, and her mantra in Sanskrit with simple pronunciation and meaning. You don't need to know anything about Hindu tradition to enjoy it.

  • 12 illustrated chaptersFrom the birth of the Goddess to her victory on the tenth day.
  • A painting for every formRich, oil-style portraits in the spirit of Raja Ravi Varma.
  • Mantras you can say aloudEach in Sanskrit, with easy pronunciation and a plain English meaning.
  • Written for everyoneSimple words, no background needed, and sources listed at the back.

Chapter 2 · Born of light

When the gods had nothing left, their light became her.

A buffalo demon named Mahishasura had won a promise that no man or god could kill him. He seized heaven and drove the gods out. In their anger, light poured from Brahma, Vishnu, Shiva and all the others, and the streams of light met and became one blaze.

Out of that blaze stepped a woman with ten arms. Each god placed his own weapon in her hands, and she smiled.

This story comes from the Devi Mahatmya, an old Sanskrit poem still recited during Dashain.

Navaratri · nine nights

Nine nights, nine forms

On each night of Navaratri one form of Durga is honoured, from the daughter of the mountain to the giver of every power. Tap a painting to read her story and her mantra.

Night 1

Shailaputriशैलपुत्री

Rides a white bull · holds a trident and a lotus

In an earlier life she was Sati, who gave up her body in the fire of her father's sacrifice. She was born again as the daughter of Himavan, king of the Himalaya, and so she is called Shailaputri, the mountain's daughter.

The nine nights begin with her, because every journey needs firm ground under it.

Her mantraॐ देवी शैलपुत्र्यै नमःOm Devi Shailaputryai NamahOm, I bow to the Goddess Shailaputri.

Night 2

Brahmachariniब्रह्मचारिणी

Walks barefoot · holds prayer beads and a water pot

To win Shiva as her husband, the young Parvati gave up every comfort and lived in the forest for years, first on fruit, then on leaves, and at last on nothing at all.

Brahmacharini is that steady, quiet devotion. She carries no weapon, only her prayer beads and a little water.

Her mantraॐ देवी ब्रह्मचारिण्यै नमःOm Devi Brahmacharinyai NamahOm, I bow to the Goddess Brahmacharini.

Night 3

Chandraghantaचन्द्रघण्टा

Rides a lion · ten arms · a bell-shaped half-moon on her brow

After she married Shiva, she wore a crescent moon on her forehead shaped like a temple bell, and that is where her name comes from.

She is calm and lovely to look at, yet every one of her ten hands is ready. The sound of her bell is said to drive fear away.

Her mantraॐ देवी चन्द्रघण्टायै नमःOm Devi Chandraghantayai NamahOm, I bow to the Goddess Chandraghanta.

Night 4

Kushmandaकूष्माण्डा

Rides a tiger · eight arms · lives at the heart of the sun

Before anything existed there was only darkness. Then she smiled, and the whole universe came out of that smile like light out of a seed.

She is said to live inside the sun itself. The warmth we feel each morning is hers.

Her mantraॐ देवी कूष्माण्डायै नमःOm Devi Kushmandayai NamahOm, I bow to the Goddess Kushmanda.

Night 5

Skandamataस्कन्दमाता

Sits on a lotus · holds baby Skanda and two lotus flowers

Her son Skanda, also called Kartikeya, grew up to lead the army of the gods. Here he is still a child with six small faces, sitting safely in his mother's lap.

She reminds us that great strength often begins with a mother's care.

Her mantraॐ देवी स्कन्दमातायै नमःOm Devi Skandamatayai NamahOm, I bow to the Goddess Skandamata.

Night 6

Katyayaniकात्यायनी

Rides a lion · holds a sword and a lotus

She was born in the hermitage of the sage Katyayana, who had prayed for years that the Goddess would come to him as his daughter.

Old stories tell of the young women of Vrindavan bathing in the Yamuna at dawn and praying to her. She is quick, fearless, and close to anyone who calls on her.

Her mantraॐ देवी कात्यायन्यै नमःOm Devi Katyayanyai NamahOm, I bow to the Goddess Katyayani.

Night 7

Kalaratriकालरात्रि

Rides a donkey · dark skin, wild hair, fire in her breath

Kalaratri means the dark night of time. She is the fiercest of the nine, with flames for breath and nothing soft about her.

Yet she is also called Shubhankari, the one who brings good, because all of her darkness is turned against what frightens us.

Her mantraॐ देवी कालरात्र्यै नमःOm Devi Kalaratryai NamahOm, I bow to the Goddess Kalaratri.

Night 8

Mahagauriमहागौरी

Rides a white bull · holds a trident and a small drum

After her years of penance, Parvati's skin was dark with dust and sun. Shiva washed her in the waters of the Ganga, and she shone as white as the moon and jasmine.

Mahagauri is the calm after the storm, gentle and quick to forgive.

Her mantraॐ देवी महागौर्यै नमःOm Devi Mahagauryai NamahOm, I bow to the Goddess Mahagauri.

Night 9

Siddhidatriसिद्धिदात्री

Sits on a lotus · holds a discus, conch, mace and lotus

A siddhi is a perfect power, and she is the one who grants them. Gods, sages and even demons bow before her.

It is said that Shiva himself received his powers from her, and that half of his body became hers. With her, the nine nights are complete.

Her mantraॐ देवी सिद्धिदात्र्यै नमःOm Devi Siddhidatryai NamahOm, I bow to the Goddess Siddhidatri.

The tenth day · Vijaya Dashami

Mahishasuramardiniमहिषासुरमर्दिनी

For nine days and nights she fought Mahishasura, a demon who could change from buffalo to man and back again. On the tenth day she struck him down while the gods showered her with flowers.

That day is Vijaya Dashami, the day of victory, and the great day of Dashain.

Day 10 · Vijaya Dashami

Mahishasuramardiniमहिषासुरमर्दिनी

Rides a lion · ten arms · strikes down the buffalo demon

For nine days and nights she fought Mahishasura, a demon who could change from buffalo to man and back again. On the tenth day she struck him down while the gods showered her with flowers.

That day is Vijaya Dashami, the day of victory, and the great day of Dashain.

Her mantraॐ महिषासुरमर्दिन्यै नमःOm Mahishasuramardinyai NamahOm, I bow to her who defeated Mahishasura.
